1. Pembroke Welsh Corgi: Playful spirit,
loyal heart, a forever friend.

Taxonomy: Canis lupus familiaris
    Origins: Descended from spitz-type herding dogs likely brought to Wales by Flemish weavers around the 12th century.
    Morphology: Low-set, sturdy dogs with short legs and long bodies. They either have naturally short "bob" tails or traditionally docked tails. Coats are medium-length and can be red, sable, fawn, or black and tan (with or without white markings).
    Purpose: Historically used as cattle herders, their low stature allowed them to nip at heels while avoiding kicks. Today, they primarily excel as beloved companion animals.
    Temperament: Pembrokes are known for their intelligence, eagerness to please, and affectionate, playful personalities. They have strong herding instincts and may try to herd children or other pets.

2. Cardigan Welsh Corgi: From farmhand to best friend.

Taxonomy: Canis lupus familiaris
    Origins: Likely share ancestry with the Dachshund and possibly the Swedish Vallhund. Developed in Cardiganshire, Wales for herding cattle.
    Morphology: Similar to Pembrokes but slightly larger in height and weight. Tails are long and flowing. They boast a wider variety of coat colors with brindle, merle, and sable patterns in addition to the typical Pembroke colors.
    Purpose: Originally versatile farm dogs used for herding and guarding. Like Pembrokes, they are now cherished primarily for companionship.
    Temperament: Cardigans are intelligent, devoted, and somewhat more reserved than Pembrokes. They possess similar herding instincts and are known for their problem-solving abilities.

Health Considerations (Both Breeds)

Intervertebral Disc Disease (IVDD): Their long backs make them predisposed to spinal issues.
    Degenerative Myelopathy (DM): A progressive neurological disease.
    Hip Dysplasia: A joint concern.

Important Note: Responsible breeders screen for health concerns to improve the overall health within both breeds.
