Other Names: Cavvies, Cavaliers, Comforter Spaniels (reflecting their role as lapdogs)

Origins and Purpose: Their ancestors were small toy spaniels depicted in European art as far back as the 16th century. Initially, they served as companions for royalty and the wealthy, prized for their gentle temperament and ability to warm laps. They were also thought to attract fleas away from their owners! Over time, they were crossed with flat-nosed breeds, leading to the modern Cavalier's distinctive features.

Temperament:  Blenheim Cavaliers are known for their sweet, gentle, and playful nature. They crave human companionship and do best in homes where they aren't left alone for extended periods. They typically get along well with children and other pets, displaying little aggression.

Vocalization: Cavaliers are less prone to excessive barking compared to some other small breeds. However, they still possess typical dog vocalizations like barks, whines, and growls for communication.

Health Considerations: Unfortunately, Cavalier King Charles Spaniels, including the Blenheim variety, are prone to certain health concerns:

Mitral Valve Disease (MVD): A common heart condition affecting many Cavaliers.
Syringomyelia (SM): A neurological condition impacting the spinal cord that can cause pain.
Eye Issues: They can have a heightened risk of eye problems like cataracts.
